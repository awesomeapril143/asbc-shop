The myth that costs small businesses contracts

The biggest myth in federal contracting: that the cheapest bid wins. Federal buyers actually prefer the lowest-risk price that meets the spec. Bid too low and they assume you can't deliver. Bid too high and you're out of range. The wins go to the bidders who price competitively, justify their numbers, and demonstrate past performance.

This kit covers the full pricing strategy section so you stop leaving money on the table — or losing the contract for being "too low to be credible."

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be in a specific industry to win federal contracts?

No. The government buys nearly everything — from cybersecurity to janitorial services, from training to construction to IT. The kit walks you through NAICS code selection so you can identify the categories where you qualify.

Does this work for state and local contracts too?

Yes. The kit includes a section on state and local programs — Texas HUB, CMBL, City of Austin set-asides — with examples that translate to most states' equivalent programs.

How long does it take to start winning contracts?

Realistic timeline: 30–60 days to set up SAM.gov, NAICS codes, capability statement, and certifications. Another 60–180 days to win your first contract. Set-aside certifications dramatically shorten the second window because you compete in smaller pools.
