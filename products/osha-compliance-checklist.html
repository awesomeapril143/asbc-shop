From real audit experience: (1) required postings are out of date or missing; (2) the HazCom binder either doesn't exist or hasn't been updated since the last chemical changed; (3) training logs show dates but no signatures, topics, or proof of understanding.

Frequently asked questions

Is this legal advice?

No. This workbook is an educational tool. For interpretation of specific OSHA standards as applied to your workplace, consult an OSHA-certified compliance officer or attorney.

Does this cover construction?

The workbook focuses on General Industry standards (1910). Construction-specific standards (1926) overlap significantly but include additional fall-protection and trade-specific requirements not covered in depth here.

Can I use this in California, New York, or other state-plan states?

Yes — the workbook covers federal OSHA. State-plan states (CA, NY, MI, OR, etc.) often have stricter requirements layered on top. Use this as your federal baseline and consult your state's plan for additional rules.
