OSHA Required Postings: What Small Businesses Must Display in 2026

Missing one of these is among the most common small-business OSHA citations. The fix is free.

Most small business owners don't fail OSHA inspections because they're unsafe. They fail because they're missing — or running outdated versions of — required workplace postings.

Every employer covered by federal OSHA must display these where employees can read them. Most are free downloads from federal agency websites. None of them require a specialty service.

1. OSHA "Job Safety and Health: It's the Law" poster

The federal OSHA poster (3165) — required at every covered workplace. Free download from osha.gov. Display it in a common employee area like a break room or near the timeclock.

2. EEO "Know Your Rights" poster

Replaced the old "EEO Is the Law" poster in 2022. Required by the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission for employers with 15+ employees (more for some categories). Free download from eeoc.gov. Available in English and Spanish.

3. FLSA Minimum Wage poster (WH-1088)

Federal minimum wage rights notice required by the Fair Labor Standards Act. Free from dol.gov. Update whenever federal or state minimum wage changes.

4. FMLA poster (Family and Medical Leave Act)

Required for employers with 50+ employees within 75 miles of the worksite. Free download. Even if you don't currently meet the threshold, having the poster up doesn't hurt.

5. USERRA poster (Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act)

Required at all worksites. Free download. Covers reemployment rights for employees who serve in the military.

6. EPPA poster (Employee Polygraph Protection Act)

Required at all worksites. Free download.

7. State-required postings (varies)

Each state requires additional postings on top of federal. Texas requires the Texas Payday Law notice, Workers' Comp Required Posting, and others. California requires significantly more (CFRA, Pregnancy Disability Leave, etc.). Check your state's labor department for the current list.

8. OSHA 300A Annual Summary

For most employers with 11+ employees, the OSHA 300A summary of work-related injuries and illnesses must be posted from February 1 to April 30 each year. Even if you had zero recordable incidents, you must post the form (showing zero).

9. Workers' Compensation notice

State-specific. Required wherever you have employees. Texas employers can opt out of workers' comp but must still post the "Notice of No Workers' Compensation Insurance" if so.

Where to display them

OSHA expects postings in conspicuous places where notices to employees are customarily posted — break rooms, near time clocks, employee bulletin boards. If you have multiple worksites, every site needs the postings (not just headquarters). For a primarily remote workforce, postings can be electronic but must be readily accessible.

Language requirements

If a meaningful portion of your workforce speaks a primary language other than English, OSHA expects required postings in that language too. Federal postings are commonly available in Spanish; some are available in additional languages on agency websites.

The trap with paid posting services

Companies that send mailers like "Your business is required to display the new federal labor law poster — pay $99 to receive it" are mostly selling free posters at a markup. Every required federal posting can be downloaded free from osha.gov, dol.gov, and eeoc.gov. The exception is some bundled state-specific updates from reputable services, but you can typically also get those free directly from your state labor department.
