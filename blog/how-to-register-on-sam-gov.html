How to Register on SAM.gov: Step-by-Step

SAM.gov is the gateway to federal contracts. Most small businesses lose 30 days to errors. Here's how to do it cleanly.

To compete for federal contracts, you must be registered on SAM.gov (System for Award Management) with an active UEI (Unique Entity Identifier) and CAGE code. Registration is free. The process is unforgiving — small data mismatches with IRS or Dun & Bradstreet records can stall validation for weeks.

Here's the path that works.

Step 1: Confirm your business is legally formed and IRS-recognized

Before SAM.gov, your business must:

  • Be legally registered in your state (LLC, Corporation, Partnership, or Sole Proprietorship with DBA)
  • Have an EIN (Employer Identification Number) from the IRS
  • Have an entity name that exactly matches what's on file with the IRS

Note the words "exactly matches." If your IRS records show "April Lewis Consulting LLC" and your state filing shows "April Lewis Consulting, LLC" (with a comma), SAM.gov validation can flag the mismatch and stall registration.

Step 2: Get your name and address aligned

Your legal business name and physical address must match across:

  • IRS records (use the name on your EIN confirmation letter, IRS Form SS-4)
  • State business registration
  • SAM.gov submission

Use a real physical address. PO boxes alone don't satisfy SAM.gov; you need a street address. A registered agent address can work.

Step 3: Create a Login.gov account

Go to login.gov and create an account using your business email and a strong password. Login.gov is the federal single-sign-on system. SAM.gov uses it.

Step 4: Get your UEI

Go to sam.gov. Click "Get Started" → "Sign In" → use your Login.gov credentials. Then "Get a Unique Entity ID" (no contracts) to receive a UEI without registering for federal awards yet, OR start a "Register Entity" workflow if you're going all the way.

UEI assignment is typically immediate if your business name and address match Dun & Bradstreet records. Otherwise, expect a few days for validation.

Step 5: Complete the full Entity Registration

The full registration includes:

  • Core Data — entity info, ownership, banking for receiving federal payments (provide your business EFT details)
  • Assertions — small business size by NAICS, products and services you offer
  • Representations & Certifications — federally-required reps and certs (these become the "FAR Part 52" attestations on contracts)
  • Points of Contact — designate Electronic Business POC, Government Business POC, Past Performance POC

Step 6: Wait for validation

SAM.gov validates registration data with IRS and other federal databases. Clean registrations validate in 7–10 business days. Mismatches can take 30–60 days to resolve. The most common mismatches:

  • Business name punctuation differs from IRS records
  • Address format differs (e.g., "Suite" vs "Ste")
  • EIN tied to a different name than the entity
  • Legal entity type misrepresented

Step 7: Get your CAGE code

CAGE (Commercial and Government Entity) code is assigned automatically by the Defense Logistics Agency once your SAM registration validates. No separate application needed. CAGE assignment usually happens within the same window as full SAM activation.

Step 8: Renew annually

SAM.gov registration expires annually. Federal payment systems will reject your invoices if registration lapses. Set a calendar reminder for 60 days before your expiration.

What to AVOID

  • Don't pay third parties to do this for you. Companies charge $300–$1,200 to do SAM registration. It's free if you do it yourself. Some "third-party SAM registration" services are outright scams.
  • Don't skip the Reps & Certs. They become legally binding on every federal contract you bid.
  • Don't list "the entire United States" as your primary place of performance if you actually only deliver remotely or locally. That can disqualify you for HUBZone or location-tied set-asides later.

What comes next

After SAM.gov is active: pursue set-aside certifications (WOSB, EDWOSB, 8(a), HUBZone, SDVOSB) at certify.sba.gov, build a federal-grade capability statement, and start hunting solicitations on SAM.gov, ESBD, eBuy, and agency forecast pages.
