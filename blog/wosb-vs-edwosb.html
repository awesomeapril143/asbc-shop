WOSB vs EDWOSB: Which SBA Certification Should You Apply For?

Both unlock federal set-aside contracts. EDWOSB unlocks more — but has stricter requirements. Here's how to choose.

If you're a woman-owned small business pursuing federal contracts, you've likely run into both terms: WOSB (Women-Owned Small Business) and EDWOSB (Economically-Disadvantaged Women-Owned Small Business). The SBA grants both certifications — and most owners are unsure which one applies to them.

Short answer: if you can qualify for EDWOSB, apply for it. EDWOSB unlocks more set-aside contracts than WOSB alone, and the application is the same form.

What WOSB and EDWOSB have in common

Both certifications require:

  • Small business under SBA size standards for your primary NAICS code
  • At least 51% directly and unconditionally owned by one or more women who are U.S. citizens
  • Woman owner holds the highest officer position (CEO, President, Managing Member)
  • Woman owner manages the day-to-day operations
  • Woman owner has unconditional control over long-term and day-to-day decisions
  • Active SAM.gov registration with a UEI

What's different about EDWOSB

EDWOSB adds three economic-disadvantage requirements that WOSB does not have:

  1. Personal Net Worth (PNW) under $850,000. Excludes equity in your primary residence, equity in your primary business, and most qualified retirement accounts. Includes investments, second homes, vehicles, etc.
  2. Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) averaged over the past 3 years under $400,000. Looking at the woman owner's tax returns.
  3. Total assets under $6.5 million. Including business assets the owner controls.

If you meet all three, you can self-certify as economically disadvantaged. EDWOSB-eligible businesses can compete for both EDWOSB-only set-asides AND general WOSB set-asides — strictly more contracts than WOSB alone.

Why EDWOSB is the better certification when you qualify

SBA designates certain procurements as "EDWOSB sole-source" or "EDWOSB set-aside" — these are restricted to EDWOSB-certified businesses. Plain WOSB-certified businesses can't compete for them. The reverse is not true: EDWOSB-certified businesses can compete for both.

In dollar terms, EDWOSB unlocks roughly $5–10 billion in additional federal contracts that WOSB alone cannot access.

When WOSB-only makes sense

If your Personal Net Worth is over $850K, your AGI is over $400K, or your total assets are over $6.5M, you don't qualify for EDWOSB — you must apply for WOSB only. That's still valuable. WOSB unlocks $20–25B annually in federal set-asides.

You can also drift between the two over time. If your income or net worth grows past EDWOSB thresholds, you'll lose EDWOSB but retain WOSB.

The application process is the same

Both certifications go through certify.sba.gov. The form asks the EDWOSB questions only if you indicate you want to apply for it. There's no penalty for applying for both — and there's no separate fee.

The most common mistake

Owners with $700K PNW and $300K AGI often think "I'm doing too well to be 'disadvantaged.'" That's a misreading. The thresholds are absolute SBA limits, not subjective standards. If you're under, you qualify. Apply.
