The Capability Statement Format Federal Buyers Actually Want

Most small business capability statements look like brochures. Here's what federal buyers expect — and the format that wins contracts.

The capability statement is the document that decides whether a federal contracting officer takes you seriously. They look at it for 30 seconds. Either they file it under "potential vendor for NAICS X" or they delete it.

Most small business capability statements get deleted because they look like marketing brochures. Federal buyers don't want a brochure — they want a structured document they can scan in 30 seconds and reference later when they have a need.

What a federal-grade capability statement contains

Five sections, in this order, on one page (two pages max):

1. Company Snapshot (top of page)

  • Company name + DBA if applicable
  • UEI (Unique Entity Identifier — from SAM.gov)
  • CAGE Code
  • Primary NAICS code(s) — bolded
  • Set-aside certifications (WOSB, EDWOSB, 8(a), HUBZone, SDVOSB, SBA Small Business)
  • State and local certifications (HUB, DBE, WBE, MBE, etc.)
  • Year established
  • Geographic service area

This block is the "is this vendor relevant to me?" filter. Make it scannable.

2. Core Competencies (3–6 bullet points, action-verb led)

What you do, in their language. Not "innovative leadership solutions." Specific. "Onboarding curriculum design for first-line supervisors. OSHA 10 and OSHA 30 training delivery. SBIRT-aligned sensitivity training facilitation."

3. Past Performance (3–5 entries)

Each entry should include:

  • Client name (federal/state/local agency or prime contractor)
  • Contract value range or actual value
  • Period of performance
  • Brief description of work
  • Reference contact (name, title, phone, email — with permission)

If you don't have federal past performance yet, list commercial clients in the same NAICS or relevant scope. Note that as commercial.

4. Differentiators (what makes you not interchangeable)

What's true about you that's not true about every other small consultancy in your space? Texas-certified WBE/DBE? OSHA + FEMA + insurance certified founder? Industry-specific experience? Multilingual delivery? List 2–4. Be specific.

5. Contact (single named person)

One name, title, phone, email. Not "info@" or "contact us." Federal buyers want a person they can call.

Format rules

  • One page (two pages absolute maximum). Federal buyers don't read longer.
  • Letter-size, white background, professional but not stylized.
  • Use your logo at the top, but don't make this a marketing piece.
  • No stock photos. Buyers find them performative.
  • PDF format. Filename should include your company name and the year (e.g., asbc-capability-statement-2026.pdf).
  • Update at minimum annually. Federal buyers notice when the dates are stale.

What to AVOID

  • Marketing language ("we deliver excellence," "industry-leading")
  • Acronyms not standard in federal contracting
  • Long paragraphs (use bullets)
  • "About Us" sections — they take space and don't help the buyer
  • Stock photos of "diverse team in conference room"
  • Sub-bullet hierarchies (keep flat)

Where to send it

Three places: (1) Email signature link, (2) SAM.gov vendor profile attachment, (3) submitted directly to agency small business specialists when you reach out about set-aside opportunities. Most federal contracting officers also accept capability statements via standard agency email when you respond to RFIs (Requests for Information).
