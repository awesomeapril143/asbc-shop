Set-Aside Contracts Decoded

WOSB, EDWOSB, 8(a), HUBZone, SDVOSB explained — plus the state and local programs most small businesses never apply for.

The U.S. federal government spends roughly $759 billion on contracts annually. About 23% — $174 billion — is reserved for small businesses. Within that small business set-aside, specific subcategories are reserved further: certain billions only go to women-owned businesses, certain billions only to veteran-owned, certain billions only to businesses in specific zones.

If you qualify for one of these subcategories and you're not certified, you're voluntarily competing against more vendors than you have to.

Here's what each one is, who qualifies, and how to apply.

WOSB — Women-Owned Small Business

Who qualifies: 51%+ owned and controlled by U.S. citizen women, who run daily operations, in a small business under SBA size standards.
What it unlocks: Roughly $20–25B of WOSB-set-aside contracts annually.
How to apply: Free, at certify.sba.gov. Processing 60–90 days for clean applications.
Renewal: Annual self-attestation; 3-year recertification.

EDWOSB — Economically-Disadvantaged Women-Owned Small Business

Who qualifies: All WOSB criteria PLUS Personal Net Worth under $850K, AGI averaged over 3 years under $400K, total assets under $6.5M.
What it unlocks: EDWOSB-only set-asides PLUS all WOSB set-asides (i.e., strictly more than WOSB alone). Roughly $5–10B of additional contracts.
How to apply: Same form as WOSB at certify.sba.gov; just check the EDWOSB box. Free.
Take this if you qualify. Use the free eligibility checker to confirm.

8(a) Business Development

Who qualifies: Small business owned 51%+ by U.S. citizens who are "socially and economically disadvantaged." Personal Net Worth under $850K, AGI under $400K. Race-based presumptions of social disadvantage exist for certain groups; others must demonstrate it.
What it unlocks: $30B+ annually in 8(a)-only contracts. Sole-source contract authority up to $4.5M (services) / $7M (manufacturing).
How to apply: Free, at certify.sba.gov. Processing 90–120 days.
Term: 9-year program, one-time only.

HUBZone — Historically Underutilized Business Zone

Who qualifies: Small business with principal office in a designated HUBZone AND 35%+ of employees living in a HUBZone.
What it unlocks: 3% federal contract goal, with sole-source authority up to $4.5M / $7M.
How to apply: Free, at certify.sba.gov. Includes a residence verification step.
Catch: HUBZone status is fragile. If you move offices or your employee residency mix changes, you can lose certification.

SDVOSB — Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business

Who qualifies: Small business 51%+ owned by service-disabled veteran(s) with documented disability rating from VA, who run daily operations.
What it unlocks: 3%+ of federal contracts (VA targets higher), plus VA's specific SDVOSB set-aside program.
How to apply: Through VA's Center for Verification and Evaluation (CVE) — being unified with SBA's certify.sba.gov over time.

VOSB — Veteran-Owned Small Business

Similar to SDVOSB but without the disability requirement. Mostly relevant for VA contracting.

State and local set-asides

Each state operates its own programs:

  • Texas HUB — Historically Underutilized Business. State spent $4.7B with HUB-certified vendors last year. (Note: HUB program restructuring in late 2025 — current status varies; check Texas Comptroller for latest.)
  • Texas CMBL — Centralized Master Bidders List. Free state-level vendor registration.
  • City of Austin DBE / WBE / MBE / WBE — local procurement set-asides.
  • Most major cities and counties have their own DBE/MBE/WBE programs.

Which one(s) should you pursue?

Stack them. There is no penalty for holding multiple certifications. Most successful small businesses pursuing federal work hold:

  • WOSB OR EDWOSB (if applicable)
  • 8(a) (if eligible — limited time window)
  • HUBZone (if their office and team residency qualify)
  • SDVOSB / VOSB (if veteran-owned)
  • 1–2 state-level certifications (HUB, DBE, WBE)

The applications take hours, not weeks. Each one is a key to a different door.
