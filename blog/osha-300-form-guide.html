OSHA 300, 300A, 301: Recordkeeping Forms Explained

OSHA recordkeeping confuses every small business owner. Here it is in plain English.

If your business has more than 10 employees and is in a covered industry, you're required to maintain three OSHA recordkeeping forms. Most small business owners have heard of them, few have seen them, and most of those who have seen them aren't sure when to fill out which one.

Here's the simple version.

OSHA Form 301 — "Injury and Illness Incident Report"

What it is: A one-page form you fill out for each individual recordable workplace injury or illness. Captures details about the affected employee, the incident, the injury, and the treatment.
When you fill it out: Within 7 calendar days of learning about the recordable injury or illness.
What "recordable" means: Generally — any work-related injury/illness resulting in death, days away from work, restricted work, transfer to another job, medical treatment beyond first aid, or loss of consciousness. Cuts treated with a band-aid are NOT recordable. Stitches ARE recordable.

OSHA Form 300 — "Log of Work-Related Injuries and Illnesses"

What it is: A running log for the calendar year. Each row is one recordable incident. Columns capture employee, job title, date, where it happened, description, and outcome (death, days away, restricted, transferred, etc.).
When you update it: As incidents happen — within 7 days of each.
How long you keep it: 5 years after the end of the calendar year it covers.

OSHA Form 300A — "Summary of Work-Related Injuries and Illnesses"

What it is: A one-page annual summary derived from your 300 log. Totals: number of recordable incidents, days away, restricted, transferred, and what types of incidents (skin disorders, respiratory, hearing loss, etc.).
When you complete it: By February 1 each year, summarizing the prior calendar year.
When to post it: February 1 through April 30. You must post it conspicuously, even if you had ZERO recordable incidents (the form will show all zeros).
When to submit it: If you have 250+ employees in any covered industry, OR 20–249 employees in certain higher-hazard industries (construction, manufacturing, healthcare, etc.), you must electronically submit the 300A to OSHA via the Injury Tracking Application (ITA) by March 2 each year.

Who is exempt

Two main exemption categories from the 300/300A/301 requirements:

  1. Small employers: Businesses with 10 or fewer employees throughout the entire prior calendar year.
  2. Partially exempt industries: Many service industries (retail, finance, real estate, certain professional services) are exempt unless OSHA notifies them otherwise. The full list is in 29 CFR 1904 Subpart B Appendix A.

Even if exempt, you must still report any work-related fatality (within 8 hours) and any work-related hospitalization, amputation, or loss of an eye (within 24 hours).

The most common mistakes

  1. Not posting the 300A from Feb 1 to April 30. This is one of the easier OSHA citations to receive. Post it visibly, leave it up.
  2. Treating the 300 as a calendar tracker rather than a real-time log. Update within 7 days of each recordable.
  3. Misclassifying "recordable." A surprising number of incidents qualify. When in doubt, log it. You can also seek guidance from an OSHA-certified consultant or your state OSHA office.
  4. Confusing the 300A summary with state-required postings. State workers' comp postings are separate. The 300A is federal. Both may be required.
  5. Forgetting electronic submission. If your industry/size triggers ITA reporting, missing the March 2 deadline is a citation.

Where to get the forms

Free PDFs at osha.gov/recordkeeping/forms. Don't pay anyone for these forms.
